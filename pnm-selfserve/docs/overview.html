PnM · MBR & ProdOps automation · prototype

Ask your MBR metrics in plain English —
without letting the AI guess the numbers.

A self-serve layer over the exact same Snowflake logic that already feeds Porter's PnM Monthly Business Review. The point isn't a chatbot that does math — it's a way to remove the analyst-in-the-loop for routine metric questions while keeping the numbers as trustworthy as the sheet.

The counter-intuitive core: the AI is the translator and the investigator, not the calculator. Numbers come from one reviewed, read-only SQL query per metric. The AI turns your question into a menu choice, gathers the evidence, and narrates the answer with its caveats — the part humans burn hours on. That division is exactly what makes it safe to point at leadership-facing numbers.
What it replaces

The manual loop today

Analyst-in-the-loop

Every "what was TPO in May?" is a ticket to someone who can write SQL, wait for a warehouse run, and paste it back.

Weekly rebuild

The MBR is reconciled by hand from brain files and pipeline output each cycle — caveats re-typed, not queried.

Hidden data debt

Definitions carry unverified table/column names. Nobody notices until a number looks wrong in a review.

The core idea

One question, traced through the system

Follow "How many tickets per order did we get in May?" from the person to the answer. Notice how few steps are actually AI — and that none of them touch the arithmetic.

Understand the question

Maps free text → a catalogued metric id + month: tpo_overall, 2026-05. If it asks for something off-menu (a city, a week, a median), it refuses instead of improvising.

Engine

Look up & gate

The registry is the single menu. Unknown metric, blocked section, or future month → hard stop. Closed-world by design.

Engine

Render one read-only SQL

The staging logic, inlined as CTEs — byte-for-byte what will run. A guard rejects anything that isn't a single SELECT.

Gate

Dry-run by default → you approve

It prints the SQL and stops. Production is touched only on an explicit --execute. No silent queries, ever.

Engine

Run the single SELECT

Read-only. Ratios rebuilt from raw numerator ÷ denominator — never averaged. Result logged to an audit trail.

AI

Narrate with a trust footer

Answer + provenance, month-basis, MTD-vs-locked, readiness, and every verify-flag verbatim. The caveat work the MBR does by hand — automatic.

Where the AI leverage actually kicks in

Four places — and the biggest win so far isn't the chat interface, it's the investigation.

01 · self-serve

English → governed metric

A stakeholder or analyst asks in their own words; the AI resolves it to a reviewed metric and month. No SQL, no waiting on a person.

Removes the analyst-in-the-loop for routine ad-hoc pulls.
Proven in iter 1–2 02 · investigation

Data archaeology & audit

The AI cross-read the Data Catalog, Metabase card #30311 and dbt lineage and caught: a ticket table that doesn't exist, an orders table missing the columns the query needs, and a methodology drift — before a single number was quoted.

Hours-to-days of analyst forensics, done up front.
03 · governance

Trust footer, automatically

Every answer ships with source, freshness, MTD labeling, readiness, and unresolved flags attached — the manual reconciliation and caveating MBR prep does today.

Turns caveats into metadata, not memory.
04 · endgame

MBR becomes queries

Once sections are validated and governed, the monthly review is generated from queries instead of rebuilt from prose and brain files each cycle.

The review stops being a rewrite, starts being a read.
Why it's trustworthy

What stays deterministic — on purpose

The AI is fenced out of anything that decides a number. These are not AI steps:

SQL generation
One reviewed query per section. The AI never writes it.
Read-only + dry-run
No writes; nothing executes without your go-ahead.
Closed-world refusal
Off-menu questions are declined, not improvised.
Readiness gating
Only you promote a section to stakeholder-ready.
