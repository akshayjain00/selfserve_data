Which Architecture Ships?

We set out to put a knowledge base of metric definitions into the hands of PMs and the business team, so they could answer their own data questions. Three designs were proposed. Two independent reviews killed two of them. This is where it actually stands.

Status as of 14 Aug 2026 Decisions D-001 → D-032 4 research agents · 2 blind reviews
Nothing approved

There is no architecture cleared to build today. The design chosen on 14 Aug was sent to an independent reviewer, which found two fatal problems and judged it worse than the design it replaced. A third approach is on the table and needs your decision. Separately — and more importantly — two questions about what our metrics even mean are unanswered, and no design can proceed past them.

The sixty-second version

If you read nothing else on this page, read these five boxes.

What we wanted

Let a PM or a business colleague ask “what was fulfilment by city last week?” and get a trustworthy number — without waiting on an analyst.

What we found

Most of the machinery already exists inside Porter. The genuinely missing piece is much smaller than expected: a way to guarantee every number is tied to an agreed definition.

What went wrong

Both designs we wrote assumed a problem that isn’t the real one. The real problem is that Porter’s dashboards already carry several conflicting definitions of the same metric.

What’s proposed now

Stop building a new place to look. Instead write the agreed definition onto the dashboards people already open, and retire the duplicates.

What’s actually blocking

Two definitional questions only you can settle — what counts as a “week”, and what goes on the bottom of the fulfilment fraction. No software fixes these.

What needs you

Two of these unblock the work. The third is a choice about how to spend the first week.

1 · Settle the week convention

Saturday→Friday, or Sunday→Saturday? Both are running in production right now and the knowledge base explicitly refuses to pick.

Until this is settled, every weekly figure any design produces is a coin-flip presented as a fact.

2 · Settle the fulfilment denominator

All orders, or only those that reached a final state? The weekly report and the knowledge base currently disagree, and the gap entry says outright that no amount of investigation settles it — it’s a choice.

This is the number most likely to reach leadership and be wrong.

3 · Pick the approach

Option G — annotate the dashboards people already use. Cheapest, needs no install, puts the governance where the traffic is. Not yet independently reviewed.

Option F with fixes — keep the card collection and repair what the review found.

Or pause — settle 1 and 2 first, then choose with better information.

Plain-language glossary

Terms used above, and what they actually mean here.

Knowledge base
A set of plain-text files recording what each business metric means, where the definition came from, and how much we trust it.
Metabase card
A single saved chart or table in Porter’s BI tool. A “collection” is a folder of them. Opening one is just following a web link.
Plugin / skill / add-on
A package someone installs into Claude so it gains knowledge or an ability. Installing is a deliberate act — which is why non-technical colleagues are hard to reach this way.
Blind review
A fresh reviewer is given the goal and the document, but never the author’s reasoning for why it’s right. The point is to catch errors the author’s own logic is blind to.
Denominator
The bottom of a fraction. Fulfilment is “orders completed ÷ demand” — and the argument is entirely about what counts as demand.
Grain and dimension
Grain is how finely time is sliced (daily, weekly, monthly). Dimension is how you cut it (by city, by route, by vehicle type).
Contested / blocked — owner
“Contested” means two answers are live and neither wins on evidence. “Blocked — owner” means only a person with authority can settle it; more analysis won’t.
Trigger-gated
Later stages start when a specific observable thing happens — not on a calendar date. Avoids committing to a roadmap we can’t predict.
